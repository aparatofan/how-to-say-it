# How to Say It — Claude guidance

Keep this file concise. It is project guidance, not a substitute for reading the specific lesson being edited.

## Start here

- This is a TBT teaching-content repository, not an application codebase.
- Work on the lesson/chapter named in the task. Do not read every lesson by default.
- Use targeted file reads/search for the requested expressions, exercise type, or editorial issue.
- Do not rewrite unrelated lessons or normalize the whole repository during a focused content task.
- Preserve the user's approved wording/structure unless the task explicitly asks for revision.

## Teaching scope

- Project: functional English for real-life communication, generally B1–B2 with general and business contexts.
- Favor natural, teachable English over rare or overly formal alternatives.
- Examples and dialogues should make the pragmatic meaning clear from context.
- Avoid political contexts unless specifically requested; use neutral general/business settings.
- Polish may be used where it helps learners, especially translations and teacher-facing notes.

## Assessment rules

When creating final multiple-choice assessment items:
- Use dialogue-based contexts with four options and one defensible correct answer.
- The keyed expression must fit the exact speech act and context naturally; do not force an expression because it belongs to the chapter.
- Avoid grammar or punctuation clues that reveal the answer.
- Spread correct-answer positions evenly and avoid predictable runs/patterns.
- Use fresh contexts rather than copying mind-map/examples verbatim.
- Balance coverage across the chapter instead of overtesting one expression/function.
- Keep distractors plausible enough to test meaning, while ensuring only one is pragmatically correct.

## Content editing

- Preserve useful distinctions between near-synonyms and functions (agreement, partial agreement, doubt, opinion, etc.).
- Prefer concise learner-facing explanations and short dialogues.
- When replacing or removing an expression, check references within the same lesson/chapter before finishing.
- Do not edit the binary `.xlsx` file unless the task explicitly asks for spreadsheet work.
- The existing folder is currently named `docs ` with a trailing space. Treat that path literally until a dedicated cleanup renames it; do not accidentally create a second `docs` tree during ordinary work.

## Git/context discipline

- `main` is the integration branch; use a focused branch for substantial edits.
- Keep commits scoped to one lesson/editorial task where practical.
- Prefer narrow reads over loading the whole content corpus into context.
- Do not paste whole chapters into the conversation when the relevant exercise/section is enough.
- For a new unrelated chapter or task, prefer a fresh Claude session.
- Finish with a brief note of what changed and any expressions/items that still need editorial judgment.
